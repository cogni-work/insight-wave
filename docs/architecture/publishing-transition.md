# Publishing transition: ownership and consumer inventory

Status: decided architecture baseline for roadmap issues #1943–#1951. This record is an inventory and migration contract; it changes no runtime behavior.

## Decision

`cogni-publishing` will own the source-to-deliverable publishing lifecycle: versioned brief contracts, theme lifecycle, semantic composition, HTML/PPTX rendering and verification, and the editorial narrative/copy subsystem. `cogni-workspace` will remain the horizontal owner of shared workspace state and operations: preferences and generated settings, language delivery, plugin and optional-tool discovery, MCP installation, health/diagnostics, markets, claims, project discovery, and Obsidian integration.

The transition has three hard boundaries:

1. Narrative and copywriting move together. `text-to-narrative` calls copywriter readability, while copywriter reads narrative arc and language contracts at runtime; splitting them would create a private cross-plugin cycle.
2. Theme authoring, import, validation, discovery, selection, bundled resources, and semantic-token compilation have one lifecycle owner: `cogni-publishing`. Existing saved themes remain readable in place; consuming a selected artifact does not make the consumer an authoring owner.
3. Claims, supported markets, project discovery, workspace preferences, plugin/tool setup, diagnostics, and Obsidian remain in `cogni-workspace`. They have live non-publishing consumers and no landed replacement. Age or host overlap is not evidence for removal.

No row below is provisional. “Retain” means supported by `cogni-workspace`; “move” means one canonical implementation in `cogni-publishing` with a bounded compatibility route; “remove” requires both a redundancy witness and a remaining-consumer countercheck.

## Cohesive editorial transfer contract

Rows E1–E4 are one migration unit. The boundary preserves both dependency directions:

- `text-to-narrative` invokes `skills/copywriter/scripts/readability.sh` and reads the copywriter readability band (`cogni-workspace/skills/text-to-narrative/SKILL.md:27-29,255-258`).
- Copywriter arc mode reads the narrative registry, arc contract, techniques and shared language headings at runtime (`cogni-workspace/skills/copywriter/SKILL.md:172-181`).
- The narrative output remains four ordered arc sections plus sources; the design brief remains a target-specific selection whose copy, numbers, citations, evidence status, notes and order are frozen (`cogni-workspace/skills/text-to-narrative/SKILL.md:262,296-327`).
- Consult-native briefs remain a separate direct input. They preserve Pyramid/SCQA/MECE structure and may carry presentation intent, but never require re-narration (`cogni-consult/references/publish-routing.md:44-85`).

The public replacement must therefore make the editorial assets locally resolvable inside publishing and separately normalize both the existing narrative brief and a structured direct brief. Rendering begins only after normalization/freezing; copywriter is never used after the freeze boundary to make content fit.

## Theme ownership contract

Publishing becomes the single lifecycle owner for theme authoring/import, validation, discovery/selection, saved theme representation, bundled resources, token compilation and compatibility projections. User-owned themes are not moved or overwritten. Publishing reads their optional configured location and returns the existing `theme_path`, `theme_name` and `theme_slug` handoff.

Business plugins continue to own their `design-variables.json` outputs and rendering use. That is artifact consumption, not a second theme lifecycle. During transition the workspace entry point may delegate to publishing; it must not keep a forked validator, importer, catalog or canonical theme store.

## Reconciliation and supported end state

All issue-named responsibilities are classified: narrative/readability/copywriter/arc and themes move; claims, markets, project discovery, preferences, plugin/tool/MCP setup, health and compatibility paths without safe exits remain supported; consult-native briefs remain direct; the old producerless renderer remains removed. Executable calls, dispatches, private references, configuration, artifacts, hooks, tests, manifests and user guidance are mapped to an owner and witness.

After #1951, `cogni-workspace` survives as a smaller, supported horizontal infrastructure plugin. It owns W1–W12 and no publishing implementation. `cogni-publishing` owns E1–E4 and T1–T2's canonical contracts/lifecycle plus the new composition/render/verification chain. Business plugins own their domain content and consume public publishing contracts or saved artifacts. No required decision is left for a later approval gate.

This phase removes no behavior, changes no manifest, and redesigns no unrelated subsystem. Later children must treat this record as a closed ownership boundary: a proposed deviation either supplies a concrete migrated replacement and passing witness or remains with the decided owner.
